# CLAUDE.md — ios-ui-stack-bench

A measurement study. One question: what Kotlin Multiplatform and Compose Multiplatform cost an iOS
app in size, startup, scroll smoothness and memory, against native SwiftUI, on physical iPhones.
**No library comes out of this repository and nothing goes upstream** — findings may become
JetBrains tickets later, but filing them is a non-goal. The deliverables are three apps built for
measurement, a harness, a document with its verdicts, and the raw logs behind every number.

## Where to start a session

1. [docs/research/research-architecture.md](docs/research/research-architecture.md) — the evidence
   this study starts from. Without it the items look like "do what the brief says", and the brief
   is wrong or silent in places that would cost days:
   - **The installed Xcode is a beta** (§1.2). Nothing is measured with `Xcode-beta.app`; the pin is
     the released build, by build number.
   - **C crashes at launch without `CADisableMinimumFrameDurationOnPhone`** (§1.6), and without it
     in A and B the study's own frame logger runs at 60 Hz on a 120 Hz screen.
   - **The study reads the iPhone XS only** (BRIEF.md A1.7), on iOS 18.7.3. The iPhone 17 Pro is
     B-18, a question for the owner. On iOS 26/27, A and B would get Liquid Glass and nothing turns
     it off (§1.7). On the XS, parity holds by A1.6, with the phone in Light appearance.
   - **Three apps per device on a free account, and the UI-test runner takes one** (§1.5).
     **Uninstalling every app of the Personal Team drops the developer trust**, and the phone then
     refuses to launch new installs until the owner trusts it again. Always leave one installed.
   - **The runner contaminates what it measures** (B-16, A2.2). With it attached, the app's own CPU
     and memory rise, and by different amounts per stack. So every verdict-deciding scenario runs
     with no runner, launched by `xctrace --launch`: S1, then S2p (the app scrolls itself) and S4p
     (it navigates itself). **The in-app frame logger sees only main-thread stalls**, not the render
     server's. Smoothness is Animation Hitches on S2p, and the logger on the harness's S2 is a
     cross-check.
   - **The App Store encrypts the code after upload**, and encrypted bytes do not compress. RQ1 is
     read on Apple's thinning estimate, and the encrypted worst case is printed beside it (A2.4).
   - **"Cold start" is a warm launch** in Apple's terms (§1.3); **three numbers in the brief's
     Context are misquoted** (§1.1) — cite the sources, never the Context.
   - **B is three arms since amendment A1.1** — B1 Objective-C export, B2 SKIE, B3 Swift export —
     each with its own verdict and never a best-of. The sessions were {A, C}, then runner-free
     {A, B1, B2} and {A, B3, C} (A1.5, A2.5).
   - §2 lists fifteen places where the frozen brief cannot be applied as written. All were settled
     in BRIEF.md before the first measurement (A1.4–A1.7).
2. [docs/research/source-brief.md](docs/research/source-brief.md) — the pre-registration, frozen.
   [BRIEF.md](BRIEF.md) — its record, the pins, the amendments; the operative text wherever it
   differs from the brief.
3. [backlog.md](backlog.md) — the queue, the stages, which item owns each kill criterion.

## The rule that governs everything here

> **A threshold moves before the first measurement or it does not move.**

The window was open until the first file under `logs/` other than its README. It closed on
2026-10-11 with commit `847bd43`, and BRIEF.md names it. Since then a number on the wrong side of a
line is a result, and a badly chosen threshold is a stated limitation beside the number it affected.
A change of method goes to the owner. If the owner makes it, it comes back as a late amendment in
BRIEF.md's second set: it says that it is late, and it never relaxes a threshold.

## The second rule

> **A green without its positive control is not a green.**

Every measuring item names a case whose outcome is known and shows it in the same log: the injected
50 ms stall (and a 0 ms one that must not register), a known delay before "list populated", a known
dirty allocation, a known incompressible resource for size. An instrument that cannot see a known
effect produces exactly the evidence of one that saw nothing to measure.

## What not to do

- **Do not report a simulator number.** Simulators are for development only; kill criterion 1 says
  simulator numbers are never a substitute.
- **Do not report a number without the run that produced it.** Every figure has a path into
  `logs/`; numbers in documents are regenerated by a script, never typed. A retraction is appended.
- **Do not set two numbers side by side without one line: same device, same session — yes or no.**
  A session's figures are compared inside it; a later session's repeat is printed beside, not pooled.
- **Do not tune a variant beyond its stack's idiomatic default.** Every non-default flag goes into
  `docs/flags.md` with its reason, and applies to every variant where it means anything.
- **Do not take a measurement outside the harness**, however quick the diagnostic feels, and do not
  reinstall in the middle of a session.
- **Do not report a difference inside its interval as zero.** It is inconclusive or below
  resolution, with the interval stated.
- **Do not add runs to chase significance** — kill criterion 4 closes the RQ as inconclusive instead.
- **Do not extend a budget.** Kill criterion 3's five working days are counted, not negotiated.
- **Do not edit `docs/research/source-brief.md` below its marker**, not even a typo or a wrong
  number in the Context; `make check` fails, and it should.
- **Do not let this become a library, a plugin or an upstream patch.**

## Where the work runs

Everything iOS — `xcodebuild`, Kotlin/Native framework builds for `ios*` targets, `xctrace`, the
devices — runs on the Mac, because nothing of it builds elsewhere. The Xcode is the pinned build,
`27A266a`, selected with `xcode-select`; a build script checks it and refuses another one.
`scripts/build-arms.sh` builds every arm: Release, for the simulator, unsigned. With
`DEVICE=<udid>`, it builds for the device, signed by the Personal Team in the git-ignored
`Local.xcconfig`.

**A session** is `python3 harness/session.py <plan.json>`, with the plans in `logs/b-17/plans/`.
- It installs the plan's arms and runs each scenario's batches in rotated order.
- **S1, S2p and S4p** run with no runner, launched by `xctrace --launch` (App Launch, Animation
  Hitches and Activity Monitor respectively). The exported tables, each trace's digest and the
  app's own log go into every attempt's directory.
- **S2 and S3** go through the UI tests in `harness/`: `harness/run.sh <arm> <S1|S2|S3|S4|IDLE>`.
  The runner is built with Xcode 27.0 and run from an `.xctestrun` with `DSTROOTPath` removed
  (B-04).
- It repeats invalid or thermally disturbed runs, stops on Low Power Mode or a low battery, and
  writes `runs.csv` after every attempt.
- `--rebuild <plan.json>` rebuilds `runs.csv` from the attempt directories.
- `analysis/verdicts.py` turns `runs.csv` into verdicts.

**The other recorders:**
- `harness/gate.py` is RQ0's (B-06).
- `harness/dryrun.py` is B-16's ruler and contamination check.
- `scripts/measure_size.py` (B-12) and `scripts/measure_download.py` (B-31) measure size.
- `harness/parity.sh` takes first screens (B-11).
- `harness/recording.py` records and exports for all of them. It also deletes the bundles and the
  GBs of raw `instruments*.ktrace` that `xctrace` leaves in `$TMPDIR`.

**The readers:**
- `analysis/inapp_reader.py` reads the app's log.
- `analysis/xctrace_reader.py` reads the exported tables.
- `analysis/gate_reader.py`, `dryrun_reader.py`, `parity_reader.py`, `size_report.py`,
  `download_report.py`.
- `analysis/readme_results.py` writes README.md's results block.

Each reader has a `*_control.py` in `make check`.

**On a device:** the phone stays unlocked, on charge, in Light appearance, with Auto-Lock set to
Never.

The fixture is generated by `fixtures/generate.py`, seeded, and `make check` holds its bytes. It is
never edited by hand.
The five Xcode projects are generated by `apps/generate_projects.py` from the KMP wizard's template —
edit the generator, never a `project.pbxproj`. **Development is checked on the simulator** (amendment A1.3) — that is what the simulator is for;
a simulator number is never written into a document or under `logs/`, and a pull request that used
one says so. **A run log is captured by the
Mac that drives the device**, by the session command, into `logs/b-NN/`, with the header described
in [logs/README.md](logs/README.md). `.trace` bundles are not committed; their `sha256` and their
exports are.

## Pull requests

One backlog item per pull request (the `backlog-item` loop). **The loop merges its own pull
requests, without waiting for CI** — stated by the owner on 2026-10-10, first as "on green", then the
same day "merge, don't wait for the checks". The local run is therefore the gate: `make check`, and
for anything the apps build reads `scripts/build-arms.sh` and `scripts/check-filter-vectors.sh`, all
green on the branch before it is pushed. CI — `check` for the documents on every change, `build` for
the apps only when their inputs change (`.github/workflows/apps.yaml`) — runs after the merge, and a
red run on `main` is fixed before the next item starts. What does not change: no check is weakened to
make it green; and the pull request carries
the log of the local build or simulator run its acceptance rests on; an item is `done` only when its
acceptance was exercised — on a device where it says so; a `question` item waits for a person; a
defect found on the way becomes an item of its own; a pull request opened by anything other than the
loop is not the loop's to merge.

**What is left** is in [backlog.md](backlog.md): the write-up (B-19), and two items that wait for
the owner (B-18, B-23). **Merge, do not squash, any branch that
touches `BRIEF.md` or `docs/research/source-brief.md`**: a squash keeps no trace of an edit made and
undone on the branch, which is the one thing the freeze's history check exists to see.
